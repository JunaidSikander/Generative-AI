# Instruction Tuning

**Module:** GenAI 403 | **Topic:** Training the Model

Fine-tuning a base model on (instruction → response) pairs so it reliably follows natural-language commands. This is a training-time change, not a prompt.

---

## Process Workflow

1. **Step 1: Collect**

   - Curate diverse instruction-response pairs across many tasks.

2. **Step 2: Fine-tune**

   - Train the base model on these pairs (often with SFT + RLHF).

3. **Step 3: Align**
   - Result follows instructions, generalizes to unseen prompts.

---

## Why It Matters

- **Turns a raw text-predictor into a helpful assistant**
- **Better zero-shot performance on unseen tasks**
- **Foundation that prompting techniques build on top of**

---

## What is a Prompt?

A prompt is the natural language text provided to a Large Language Model (LLM) to elicit a specific response. It acts as the programming code—the primary interface through which humans steer AI behavior.

### The Anatomy of a Perfect Prompt:

- **>\_ Instruction:** The specific task or directive you want the model to perform (e.g., "Summarize", "Translate", "Analyze").
- **Context:** External information, constraints, or background needed to ground the model correctly.
- **Input Data:** The actual raw text, code, or information to be processed.
- **</> Output Indicator:** The desired format, length, or structure of the result (e.g., JSON, markdown, bullet).

## Systematic Prompts

### The Model's Standing Instructions

A high-priority message that defines persona, scope, tone, and constraints before any user turn. It frames every response that follows.

- **Role & Persona:** "You are a senior clinical-coding assistant."
- **Boundaries:** What to refuse, escalate, or avoid.
- **Output Format:** JSON, markdown, length, language.
- **Behaviour:** Tone, level of detail, when to ask.

---

### The Message Hierarchy

---

**⚙️ SYSTEM**
Identity, rules, format — highest priority

---

**🛠️ DEVELOPER**
Task-specific guardrails & tools

---

**👤 USER**
The actual request

---

**🤖 ASSISTANT**
Model's reply, bound by the above

# Systematic Prompt Anatomy: MedCode AI

**WORKED EXAMPLE**

## Persona

You are MedCode AI, a clinical coding assistant specialising in ICD-10-CM and CPT codes.

Communicate with healthcare professionals precisely and concisely.

## Rules

- Cite code + full description
- Flag ambiguous cases clearly
- Confidence threshold: >= 0.85
- Request clarification if documentation is incomplete
- Never exceed your knowledge scope

## Output Format

Respond ONLY as valid JSON:

```json
{
  "code": "string",
  "description": "string",
  "confidence": 0.0,
  "notes": "string | null"
}
```

## Limits

- No medical advice whatsoever
- No interpretation of lab values
- No diagnoses or prognoses
- Escalate uncertain cases to a supervising physician

---

### Summary:

- Each section answers: WHO is the model (persona)
- HOW it must behave (rules)
- WHAT format to use (output)
- WHAT to refuse (limits).

## What is Prompt Engineering?

> The Science of Prompting

### Garbage In, Garbage Out

- The quality of your prompt determines the exact quality of your result.
- Rarely does a complex prompt work perfectly on the first try.

**Keep it Clear, Contextual, and Constrained.**

### Workflow Steps

1. **Define Goal**

   - Establish clear success criteria and what the optimal output looks like.

2. **Draft Prompt**

   - Apply best practices: context, clear instructions, and structured formatting.

3. **Test & Refine**
   - Evaluate outputs against criteria.
   - Iterate on instructions, add examples, or apply CoT (Chain of Thought).

## Few-shot Learning

Teach the model the task by placing examples directly in the prompt. No weight updates required.

---

### Framework

- **Zero-shot** (0 examples)

  - Just an instruction. Relies on the model's pre-trained knowledge.

- **One-shot** (1 example)

  - A single demonstration anchors the format and tone.

- **Few-shot** (2–5 examples)
  - Several diverse examples teach the pattern and edge cases.

---

### Make examples count

- Keep formatting identical across examples
- Cover edge cases
- Order matters (recency bias)
- More shots help until the context fills with noise

# Few-shot: 0-shot vs 3-shot Comparison

## ZERO-SHOT (no examples)

### PROMPT

> What is the sentiment of this restaurant review?
>
> "I ordered the salmon. It was delivered cold and tasteless."
>
> Classify it.

↓

### MODEL RESPONSE

> The sentiment is negative as the customer was disappointed.

❌ **Verbose, inconsistent format** — hard to parse reliably.

---

## 3-SHOT (with examples)

### PROMPT

> Classify review: positive/negative
>
> "Best meal I have ever had!"
> -> positive
>
> "Wrong order, never coming back."
> -> negative
>
> "Food was okay, nothing special."
> -> neutral
>
> Now classify:
> "I ordered the salmon. It was delivered cold and tasteless."

↓

### MODEL RESPONSE

> negative

✔ **Single-word label** — consistent, parseable, replicable.

## Chain-of-Thought (CoT)

### What it is

Prompt the model to **show its reasoning step by step** before giving a final answer.

- Best for arithmetic, logic & multi-hop questions
- Trigger with "Let's think step by step"
- **Few-shot CoT:** show worked examples with reasoning
- Makes errors visible and easier to debug

---

### How the reasoning flows

#### QUESTION

> "A shelf holds 3 rows of 8 books. 5 are removed. How many remain?"

↓

#### REASON

> 3 × 8 = 24 books in total.

↓

#### REASON

> 24 - 5 = 19 books remain.

↓

#### ANSWER

> **19 books.**

---

## Chain-of-Thought: Before & After

---

## ❌ WITHOUT CoT trigger

### PROMPT

> A baker makes 6 trays of cookies.  
> Each tray holds 24 cookies.  
> He sells 80 cookies.  
> How many are left?

↓

### MODEL RESPONSE

> 64 cookies.

❌ **Bare answer** — no reasoning, nothing to verify or debug.

---

## ✔ WITH CoT trigger

### PROMPT

> A baker makes 6 trays of cookies.  
> Each tray holds 24 cookies.  
> He sells 80 cookies.  
> How many are left?
>
> Think step by step. _(▲ trigger phrase)_

↓

### MODEL RESPONSE

> Step 1: 6 trays x 24 = 144 total.  
> Step 2: 144 - 80 sold = 64 remain.
>
> Answer: 64 cookies.

✔ **Steps transparent** — easy to verify, trust and debug.

## Tree-of-Thought (ToT)

---

### What it is

Explore multiple reasoning paths in parallel, evaluate each branch, then keep the most promising and backtrack from dead ends.

- Generates several candidate "thoughts" per step
- Scores branches; prunes weak ones
- Backtracks instead of committing early
- Strong on planning, search & puzzles

## Tree-of-Thought: Prompt + Model Output

---

## PROMPT TEMPLATE

> You are a creative problem-solver.
>
> **Problem:** Write a marketing tagline for an eco-friendly water bottle.
>
> Generate 3 distinct approaches:
> A. [approach one]  
> B. [approach two]  
> C. [approach three]
>
> Score each (1-5) on: novelty and audience appeal, then develop the highest-scoring approach fully.

> 💡 **Why it works:** Forcing 3 branches prevents premature commitment. Scoring before developing avoids the model anchoring on the first idea it generates.

---

## MODEL OUTPUT

### Branch A – Feature-led

- _"100% plastic-free hydration"_
- Novelty: 2 | Appeal: 3 ➔ **5**

### Branch B – Emotion-led

- _"Drink. Refill. Change the world."_
- Novelty: 4 | Appeal: 5 ➔ **9 \*\*\***

### Branch C – Data-led

- _"Save 1,000 bottles a year"_
- Novelty: 3 | Appeal: 3 ➔ **6**

---

**Selected:** Branch B (score: 9)

### Expanded

> _"Drink. Refill. Change the world."_  
> Every refill is a small act of environmental commitment. Choose a bottle that outlasts the trend.

## Chain-of-Thought vs Tree-of-Thought

| Feature         | Chain-of-Thought                  | Tree-of-Thought               |
| :-------------- | :-------------------------------- | :---------------------------- |
| **Shape**       | Single linear path                | Branching tree of paths       |
| **Exploration** | Commits to one line of reasoning  | Tries many, keeps the best    |
| **Recovery**    | Hard to recover from a wrong step | Backtracks from dead ends     |
| **Cost**        | Low — one pass                    | Higher — many calls + scoring |
| **Best fit**    | Math, logic, explanations         | Planning, search, puzzles     |

# ADVANCED PATTERN

## Stacking System + Few-shot + CoT

---

### PROMPT STACK

#### 1. SYSTEM LAYER

> **[System]** You are a Python tutor for beginners.  
> Use real-world analogies. Keep answers <= 80 words.

↓

#### 2. FEW-SHOT LAYER

> **[User]** What is a variable?  
> **[Asst]** Like a labeled jar - you put a value inside and retrieve it by the label. `x = 5` stores 5 in a jar labeled "x".

↓

#### 3. USER + CoT

> **[User]** What is recursion? Think step by step.

---

### MODEL OUTPUT

> **Step 1:** A function that calls itself.
>
> **Step 2:** Like nested Russian dolls, each doll opens to reveal a smaller one, until you reach the smallest.
>
> **Step 3:** That smallest doll is the "base case" and it stops the nesting.
>
> **Result:** Recursion solves a big problem by reducing it to smaller versions of the same problem.
